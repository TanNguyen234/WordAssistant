# Protecting Authentic Human Writing & Preventing False Positives

When auditing or reviewing text, the goal is **never** to conduct a witch hunt or penalize formal, articulate human writing. Large language models were trained on human text, so many individual patterns also appear in human writing. Accusing a human writer of using AI based solely on isolated stylistic habits can damage trust and alienate contributors.

This guide outlines clear distinctions between actual AI generation and human writing traits, preventing false positive accusations.

---

## 1. Ineffective Indicators (Things That Do NOT Prove AI)

Do not claim a text is AI-generated based on the following:

1. **Flawless Grammar or High Readability:**
   - Skilled writers, professional journalists, academics, and experienced editors naturally produce grammatically clean prose. Flawless syntax is not a violation.
2. **Academic, Technical, or Elevated Formal Tone:**
   - Scholarly writing uses formal vocabulary by necessity. While LLMs favor certain specific words (e.g. *delve*, *pivotal*, *testament*), broad academic vocabulary (e.g. *empirical*, *methodology*, *paradigm*) is standard human scholarly prose.
3. **Isolated Transition Words:**
   - Single occurrences of *Additionally*, *Consequently*, *Furthermore*, or *Tuy nhiên*, *Hơn nữa*, *Đồng thời* are standard rhetorical connectors taught in high school and university composition courses. Only mechanical, repeated sentence-initial placement signals a problem.
4. **Lack of Citations or Poor Sourcing:**
   - Hundreds of thousands of human-written Wikipedia articles and draft papers lack citations. Unreferenced text predates AI by centuries; in fact, modern LLMs with web search frequently generate dense (though sometimes confabulated) citations.
5. **Combined Informal & Formal Registers:**
   - A writer who switches between playful idioms and precise technical terms is often displaying human idiosyncrasy, personality, or neurodivergence—not an AI model.
6. **"Bland" or "Dry" Writing:**
   - Encyclopedic or bureaucratic writing is intended to be dry. Dryness is not a hallmark of AI; AI actually skews toward *unsolicited cheerfulness, puffery, and moralizing*.

---

## 2. Strong Signs of Authentic Human Writing

When evaluating ambiguous text, look for signs that point strongly toward human authorship:

### 2.1 The November 30, 2022 Hard Cutoff (Temporal Grounding)
- ChatGPT launched publicly on **November 30, 2022**.
- Any text, commit, edit, paper, or document created before this date **cannot** have been generated by conversational LLMs. If an older revision contains bold text, lists, or elevated words, it is verified human writing.

### 2.2 Direct and Unpretentious Syntax
- Human writing uses direct copulas (*is*, *are*, *was*, *has*) freely and without shame:
  - *"There is a large oak tree in the center of the park."*
  - LLMs avoid this and write: *"A large oak tree stands as the focal point of the arboreal landscape."*
- Human writing prefers ordinary, precise verbs:
  - *wrote* (vs. *authored*)
  - *used* (vs. *utilized*)
  - *moved* (vs. *relocated*)
  - *died* (vs. *passed away*)
  - *chết / mất* (thay vì *qua đời / tạ thế* khi đưa tin thông thường)
  - *dùng / làm* (thay vì *vận dụng / phát huy / khai thác*)

### 2.3 Conversational Asymmetry & Structural Variety
- Humans do not write in uniform 15–25 word sentences. Human paragraphs feature varied cadence: a short punchy sentence followed by a long, meandering dependent clause, followed by an abrupt fragment or parenthesis.
- Humans repeat a technical term 3 times in two sentences if that is the accurate word, without resorting to 5 different synonyms from a thesaurus (avoiding *elegant variation*).

### 2.4 Editorial Accountability
- When asked why a specific sentence was written or why a source was chosen, a human author can explain: *"I got that number from table 4 in the 2021 annual report, but I think I misread the column header."*
- A user who merely pasted LLM output will either fail to explain, repeat vague generalities, or produce another AI response.

## 3. Thresholds of Evidence for Labeling Text as AI

To ensure ethical and accurate audits, enforce a strict multi-factor threshold:

| Level | Evidence Required | Appropriate Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Clean / Human** | Normal human prose, isolated transitions, zero engine leaks. | Accept without qualification. |
| **Low Concern** | 1–2 cliché words (e.g. *crucial*, *then chốt*), but natural syntax. | Treat as human writing style. No action needed. |
| **Moderate Concern** | Dense clichés + 1 trailing participle + repetitive negative parallelism. | Request source verification and copyedit for conciseness; do NOT accuse of AI. |
| **High Probability** | 3+ structural tells (inline lists, puffery, weasel attribution) + dense vocabulary. | Flag for complete stylistic rewrite and source audit. |
| **Definite AI** | Engine leak (`oaicite`, `turn0search`, `【...†L...】`, `[cite: 1]`, `utm_source=chatgpt`), unedited placeholder (`[Insert ...]`), or chatbot chat phrase. | Immediate rejection / complete rewrite required. |
